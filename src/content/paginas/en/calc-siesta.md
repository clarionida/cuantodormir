## How long should a nap be?

A short nap of about 10 minutes usually brings the most immediate relief. Slightly longer naps can help too, but you’re more likely to feel a few minutes of grogginess when you wake up. If you lie down now, we’ll tell you when to set your alarm for each length.

<p class="formula">alarm time = now + time to fall asleep + nap length</p>

For example, if it’s 3:00 PM and it takes you about 15 minutes to fall asleep: for a 10-minute nap, 3:25 PM; for 18 minutes, 3:33 PM; for 25 minutes, 3:40 PM; and for a full 90-minute cycle, 4:45 PM.

In a study with young people who had slept little the night before, the 10-minute nap brought the most immediate improvements in alertness and mood. The 20- and 30-minute naps also helped, but their benefits came later, after a stretch of grogginess ([Brooks and Lack, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16796222/)). That grogginess on waking is called sleep inertia ([Hilditch and McHill, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31692489/)).

A full 90-minute cycle is an option if you were short on sleep last night and have the time. Read more in [the ideal nap](/guia/siesta-ideal/) (in Spanish) and [sleep inertia](/guia/inercia-del-sueno/) (in Spanish).
